## What Hootsuite actually costs a small team

Hootsuite's Standard plan is $99 per user per month billed annually, with ten social accounts, per [Hootsuite's pricing page](https://hootsuite.com/pricing) as of November 2026. Professional is $199 per user and Advanced is $399. There's no free plan, only a 14-day trial.

So the bill is driven by headcount. One marketer on Standard is $99. Add a designer and a founder who approves posts, and it's [about $297 a month on annual billing](https://hootsuite.com/pricing), for the same ten accounts.

> Hootsuite charges for every person who touches the calendar, including the one who only approves.

And approvals themselves sit high up the ladder. Hootsuite lists "review and approve content with your team" on Advanced, at $399 per user, and not on Standard or Professional. That's the gap the tools below fill. Most of them charge for accounts or plans instead of seats, which suits a three-person team much better than a model built for marketing departments.

### 2. Buffer

Buffer's Team plan is $12 per channel per month on monthly billing, with unlimited team members and approval workflows, per [Buffer's pricing](https://buffer.com/pricing). Pick it if you want the calmest composer here and your channel count is modest. Ten channels cost about $120 a month, still well under three Hootsuite seats.

### 3. Postiz

Postiz Team costs $39 a month for ten channels with unlimited members, per [Postiz pricing](https://postiz.com/pricing). It's open source, so you can also self-host. Pick it when price matters most and someone on the team is comfortable with a rougher edge.

### 4. SocialBee

SocialBee Pro is $99 a month for 25 profiles, with three users per workspace across five workspaces, per [SocialBee's pricing](https://socialbee.com/pricing). Pick it if you run several clients and want each in its own workspace. Below Pro, plans include one user, so a team skips straight to it.

### 5. Metricool

Metricool's Starter plan is $25 a month for up to five brands, and the free plan covers one brand, per [Metricool's pricing](https://metricool.com/pricing/). Pick it if your boss or client cares most about the monthly report. The scheduler is solid. Not the point, though.

## When Hootsuite is still the right call

Some teams should stay. Hootsuite's Enterprise plan lists advanced listening across social, digital and AI, custom compliance solutions and single sign-on, per the same [pricing page](https://hootsuite.com/pricing). If your legal team requires those, the alternatives here won't match it, and that's a fair reason to pay more.

But a three-person team scheduling posts to ten accounts rarely needs them. Is your team actually using listening every week? If not, you're paying for a department's toolkit.

A quick test helps. List what you used in Hootsuite last month. If it's scheduling, approvals and a report, any tool above covers it for a fraction of the price (and approvals alone would cost you the Advanced tier).

## FAQ

### Is there a cheaper alternative to Hootsuite?

Yes, by a wide margin for small teams. Hootsuite Standard is $99 per user per month on annual billing. Postiz Team is $39 a month for ten channels with unlimited members, Buffer Team is $12 per channel, and Krevaya Plus is $29 a month. The saving grows with headcount, because most alternatives don't charge per person.

### Does Hootsuite have a free plan?

No. Hootsuite offers a 14-day free trial on its Standard, Professional and Advanced plans, and billing starts when the trial ends unless you cancel. If you need a free tier, Metricool, Publer and Krevaya each have one, with limits on accounts or monthly posts.

### What is the best Hootsuite alternative for agencies?

SocialBee Pro is the strongest fit for client work: 25 profiles, five workspaces and three users per workspace for $99 a month. Teams that want approvals plus AI drafting should look at Krevaya. Price your real profile count on two tools before switching, since the cheapest choice changes as you add clients.

### Can I keep my Hootsuite analytics if I switch?

Export them first. Analytics history generally stays with the tool that collected it, so download the reports you need before your plan ends. Your new tool starts collecting from the day you connect each account, so a short overlap where both run keeps your month-over-month numbers continuous.
